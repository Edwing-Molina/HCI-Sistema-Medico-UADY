Here is the detailed breakdown for the **Satisfaction** attribute, following the technical format and tailored specifically for the UADY Medical Service app context (Don José and Dr. Elena).

---

## 1. Detailed Description of the Attribute

**Satisfaction** is the degree to which a user’s needs are met during and after interacting with the system. It encompasses physical comfort (lack of fatigue), cognitive ease, and emotional responses such as a sense of achievement and security.

It is divided into three main dimensions:

### Empowerment and Control
The user feels they are the master of the tool, not the other way around. This is especially critical for users with lower digital literacy.

*Example:* **Don José** feels capable of scheduling his own appointment without needing to ask his children for help, reinforcing his sense of independence.

### Systemic Trust
The confidence the user places in the system to accurately manage sensitive medical and administrative information.

*Example:* **Dr. Elena** trusts that the medical pass generated by the app is valid and that her clinical records are handled securely and accurately.

### Emotional and Physical Comfort
The absence of stress, frustration, or visual strain after completing a task. The user leaves the interaction with a positive mindset.

*Example:* A user finishes checking their beneficiaries and feels "relieved" rather than "exhausted" by the interface's complexity.

# 2. Metrics

Satisfaction is subjective and must be captured through direct user feedback and behavioral observation after the interaction.

# A. System Usability Scale (SUS) - [Manual / User-Based]

## Description
The industry standard for measuring perceived usability and satisfaction. It consists of a 10-item questionnaire that evaluates complexity, ease of use, and learnability.

## How to Measure It
Administer the standardized SUS questionnaire immediately after the user completes a core task (e.g., scheduling an appointment).

## Formula
$$SUS = \left( \sum_{n=1}^{10} normalized\_score \right) \times 2.5$$

## Questions for Evidence Analysis
- *Perceived Ease:* Did the user find the system unnecessarily complex?
- *Consistency:* Did the user feel that the various functions (appointments, records, passes) were well integrated?
- *Confidence in Use:* Did the user feel they would need the support of a technical person to be able to use this system?

## Required Numerical Values
- Responses on a 5-point Likert scale (Strongly Disagree to Strongly Agree).
- $normalized\_score$: Odd-numbered questions (score - 1); Even-numbered questions (5 - score).

## Example Application
If a group of 5 maintenance workers provides responses that, when normalized, result in a sum of 34:
$$SUS = 34 \times 2.5 = 85$$
A value **> 80** is considered "Excellent" and indicates high satisfaction.

# B. Perceived Self-Efficacy Index (PSEI) - [Manual / User-Based]

## Description
Directly measures the "Don José" dimension. It evaluates how capable the user feels of performing critical tasks on their own in the future.

## How to Measure It
Through a single-scale post-task question: *"On a scale of 1 to 5, how capable do you feel of scheduling an appointment by yourself next time?"*

## Formula
$$PSEI = \frac{\sum Puntuations}{N_{users}}$$

## Questions for Evidence Analysis
- *Sense of Mastery:* Did the user verbalize a sense of "I can do this" during the task?
- *Independence:* Did the user manage to finish the process without looking for external confirmation or help?

## Required Numerical Values
- Scale from 1 (Not capable at all) to 5 (Totally capable).

## Example Application
If 10 older adults are tested: 7 rate it a 5, and 3 rate it a 4:
$$PSEI = \frac{(7 \times 5) + (3 \times 4)}{10} = 4.7$$
Target: A value **$\ge$ 4.5** to ensure the user feels "in control."

# C. Health Management Trust Index (HMTI) - [Manual / User-Based]

## Description
Measures the "Dr. Elena" dimension. It evaluates the emotional security generated by the app when handling health-related and administrative data.

## How to Measure It
Specific trust questions after the workflow: *"How confident are you that your appointment was recorded correctly and securely?"*

## Formula
$$HMTI = \left( \frac{U_{trust}}{U_{total}} \right) \times 100$$

## Questions for Evidence Analysis
- *Data Reliability:* Does the user feel their clinical history is being presented accurately?
- *Administrative Validity:* Does the user believe the digital medical pass will be accepted at the hospital without issues?

## Required Numerical Values
- $U_{trust}$: Users who respond "Confident" or "Very Confident."
- $U_{total}$: Total number of users surveyed.

## Example Application
If 20 academic staff members test the "Pases" module and 18 express full confidence in the result:
$$HMTI = \left( \frac{18}{20} \right) \times 100 = 90\%$$

# D. Net Emotional Response (NER) - [Manual / Observation]

## Description
Evaluates physical and emotional reactions during app usage. It looks for non-verbal signs of frustration or satisfaction.

## How to Measure It
Direct observation during usability testing, recording spontaneous comments and physical gestures.

## Formula
$$NER = \frac{C_{pos}}{C_{total}}$$

## Questions for Evidence Analysis
- *Physical Frustration:* Were there signs of eye strain, moving the phone away, or sighs of frustration?
- *Verbal Satisfaction:* Did the user make positive comments such as "That was fast!" or "This is very clear"?

## Required Numerical Values
- $C_{pos}$: Count of positive reactions or comments.
- $C_{total}$: Total count of recorded reactions (positive + negative).

## Example Application
If a maintenance worker makes 5 positive comments and only 1 comment of doubt:
$$NER = \frac{5}{6} = 0.83 \text{ (83\% positive response)}$$
