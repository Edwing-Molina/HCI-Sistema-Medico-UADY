## App for Medical Service Beneficiaries

**Objective**
To simplify and digitize the procedures that Medical Service beneficiaries currently perform at service windows or via phone.

**Stakeholders**
We have two profiles with contrasting needs and levels of technological skill.

1. **Manual Users (Janitors / Maintenance Staff)**
   These are beneficiaries who access the medical service but face technological barriers, such as the lack of a Microsoft institutional email account or limited experience using complex digital platforms. They are particularly vulnerable to the digitization process. If the app requires an institutional email to function, an entire population that directly depends on the service would be excluded.
   By including them from the very beginning, we can detect "**stumbling blocks**" that would otherwise catch us off guard. Their participation helps design an interface that does not require advanced organizational skills.
2. **Teaching and Administrative Staff (Ages 45–70)**
   This profile groups users who, although they possess the necessary credentials, require a user experience (UX) approach focused on transitioning from traditional to digital systems. Since they have an active institutional email and are familiar with the Microsoft ecosystem, their initial learning curve is lower and they can adopt the app more quickly.
   The focus for this group centers on advanced, high-efficiency features, such as automated management of dependents (children and spouses). However, given the age range, the design must prioritize readability and clear navigation to mitigate visual fatigue or anxiety caused by cluttered interfaces.

**Justification for the Dual Approach**
For the purposes of this course, focusing on these two profiles allows us to apply a genuinely participatory design process. By analyzing both those with strong technical proficiency and those who face access barriers, we can avoid blind spots in the design.
