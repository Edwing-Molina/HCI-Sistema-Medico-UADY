# Reflection: Cognitive Sustainability and the Prevention of Digital Harm

**Core Idea: HCI design should function as a regulator of human well-being, prioritizing the preservation of autonomy and mental integrity over the demands of modern automation.**

## Reflection

**Claim (Main Argument):**  
Preventing physical and mental harm in digital environments needs Human–Computer Interaction (HCI) design to evolve from a traditional metric of *efficiency* to one of *cognitive sustainability*, where software protects user **homeostasis** (Ability to maintain a stable state) by reducing decision fatigue and avoiding behavioral manipulation.

**Data (Evidence):**  
Although interfaces have evolved from desktop environments to the Metaverse and Artificial Intelligence systems, The structure of human cognitive processing, attention, memory, and perception, keeps biologically stable. However, the integration of autonomous agents has introduced *automation bias*, occurs when users have less control over the system’s decisions and their perception of the situation is limited, leading to high levels of frustration and anxiety.

**Warrant (Reasoning):**  
The connection between design and health lies in the fact that systems ignoring cognitive load limits, such as the need for immediate feedback within less than one second, this disrupt the user’s workflow. This disruption can translate into physiological stress and increased operational errors.

**Backing (Additional Support):**  
The validity of this perspective is reinforced by recent studies demonstrating that structured usability frameworks still works even in government systems. These frameworks ensure that digital services are not only functional but also safe and equitable in terms of their psychological impact on the population.

**Qualifier (Limitations):**  
While many usability principles are applicable, they must continuously adapt to new technologies. In environments as VR or AI systems, methodologies must remain flexible to address ethical and trust-related challenges that were not present during earlier eras of static interface design.

**Rebuttal (Counterargument):**  
Although some popular platforms operate successfully despite deviating from the common usability standards, research indicates that inconsistencies in these tools eventually require more rigorous evaluation methods in order to avoid compromising users quality of life.

## Team Project Example: Medical Services App

In our **medical service project**, applying these principles is particularly important for the persona **Don Jose, the manual worker**. While managing functional requirements **RF01–RF16** in a medical service environment, the interface must avoid *harm caused by confusion*. For example, the system implements clear **visibility of system status** so that Don Jose never experiences uncertainty about whether a critical technical report has been successfully submitted. Reducing cognitive load in this way ensures that the tool functions as operational support over an additional source of mental stress during his workday.
