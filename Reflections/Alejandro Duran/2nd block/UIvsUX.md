# Reflection: UI and UX frequently are misunderstood because are merged into a single role

## Claim

The habitual merging of **"UI"** and **"UX"** into a single professional role is the root cause of their frequent misuse; it collapses a complex strategic process (**UX**) into a tangible output (**UI**), leading stakeholders to believe that a "pretty" product is inherently a "functional" one.

---

## Data (Evidence)

Industry data shows that a vast majority of job postings for **"UI/UX Designers"** prioritize skills in visual tools like **Figma** or **Adobe** over competencies in **cognitive psychology**, **ethnographic research**, or **information architecture**.

Academically, many **bootcamps** promise to teach both disciplines in a matter of weeks, often dedicating **80% of the curriculum to visual design** and only **20% to the invisible logic of user journeys**.

---

## Warrant

When language combines two distinct functions into one, the human brain tends to prioritize the more visible element.

Because the **User Interface** is the only part of the design that a client can actually see and touch, the **"UI"** half of the shorthand effectively cannibalizes the **"UX"** half. The shorthand creates an illusion that the **strategy of the journey** is just a byproduct of **designing the vehicle**.

---

## Backing

The reference article from the page **WPMU DEV** highlights that **UI is the "vehicle"** and **UX is the "soul" or the experience as a whole**.

In practice, if a single professional is tasked with both, they face a **conflict of interest**: the pressure to produce a **high-fidelity mockup (UI)** to demonstrate progress often comes at the expense of the rigorous, **invisible testing** required to ensure a logical flow (**UX**).

---

## Qualifier

This misunderstanding is particularly acute in **small-to-mid-sized tech environments** or **generalist freelance markets**.

In **large-scale enterprise environments** or **specialized design agencies**, the roles are often strictly separated into **User Researchers** and **Visual Designers**, which helps preserve the integrity of each discipline.

---

## Rebuttal

Critics might argue that **"UI/UX"** is a valid shorthand because the two are **symbiotic** you cannot build a great experience without a functional interface.

While this is true, **symbiosis does not mean identity**.

Just as a **chef** and a **nutritionist** both work with food, their goals are different:

- The **chef** focuses on the **sensory delight of the meal** (UI).
- The **nutritionist** focuses on the **long-term health outcome for the body** (UX).

Confusing the two leads to **"junk food" software** it looks great but provides **no lasting value**.

---

## Team Project Application: The Medical Services App

In our project, we had an initial contact with some **mockups** showing how the administrative staff of the project wanted the app to look.

We could have spent entire sprints **perfecting the color palette, the shapes of the appointment cards, and the animation of the splash screen**. This would have resulted in a **"beautiful" dashboard**.

However, we decided to evolve beyond the first mockups and ask ourselves as a group:

> *"How does a 70-year-old patient with low digital literacy find their prescription history during an emergency?"*

Answering this question required **Information Architecture (UX)**—organizing the data so the most vital information is **only one tap away**.

It also required several **discussions between the product owner and the client** to ensure the experience was **simple, accessible, and usable for everyone**.
