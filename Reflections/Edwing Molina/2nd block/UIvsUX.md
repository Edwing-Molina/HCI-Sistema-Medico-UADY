# UI vs UX

UI and UX are concepts that many people believe are the same. At first, I confused them myself. I thought that if a game or app was difficult to use, it was because the UI was bad, which could also be the case, but it was more of a user experience problem. <mark>These concepts are distinct but closely related, and it's crucial to understand the clear difference so that software development is focused on what's being sought, whether it's a good user experience or a UI that looks good and has good colors.<mark>

Nowadays, these concepts are becoming increasingly recognized and studied to understand how to create a good user experience for your systems, leading to better designs, better planning, and consideration of these factors throughout software development.

According to the article:

- UX: It's the "essence" or the "heart." It focuses on the logical and emotional satisfaction of the user. Its goal is for the site to be useful, easy to navigate, and to solve problems smoothly.

- UI: These are the physical and technical elements that enable interaction (buttons, controls, typography, icons). It is the visual representation of UX. The article defines it as the "vehicle" necessary to achieve the experience.


I've applied these concepts to my project. For example, UI is the easiest and is reflected in the UI's colors and typography, which are based on the UADY Identity Manual to ensure the app's consistency and integration with the institution's environment, just like the other systems. UX, on the other hand, is a bit more complex to implement. In our case, much of the user experience relies on users being as immersed as possible in the institution's environment. Since many of these systems are being unified into a single login that many users already employ, the login maintains the institutional colors and the same login style as Microsoft 365. Because there was already an appointment system, the same flow for scheduling and checking appointments is maintained. For the other modules not covered in this project, the goal is a simple user experience for these new modules and ease of use for the requests module (still under development).

In conclusion, these concepts are very similar but also different, which is why they are often confused. However, the most difficult to implement in development is UX, so we must learn how to include and implement them in our systems, since it is often these UX details that make our apps very popular or that no one wants to use.
