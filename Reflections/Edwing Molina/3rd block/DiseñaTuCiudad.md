# Diseña Tu Ciudad Reflection.

In my experience, having been involved three times now, I’ve noticed improvements over time. However, the same issue always persists: the registration process can be very confusing for people who lack technological access or literacy. Additionally, the registration system frequently crashes due to high demand. While this might not be a usability issue per se, it directly impacts user satisfaction; when people see the system failing and can’t complete their registration, they give up—this is especially common among older adults.

On election day, it is very rare—or at least I haven't seen it—for someone to register on their own while at the module. Almost everyone waits for someone else to register them because they either don't know how or simply find it too tedious to do themselves. The recent shift to ID scanning introduced even more problems. While it often allowed the registration to be completed, the system wouldn't validate it correctly. Consequently, when people went to vote, the QR code would fail, requiring it to be regenerated. There was also the issue of voters not knowing their local electoral district; they would either assume the one shown in the registry was correct or assign it incorrectly, leading to the wrong options appearing on their ballot.

Overall, the registration platform is the system with the most accessibility and usability flaws. In an attempt to make it fast, security is sacrificed. This isn't unique to this system; it’s a global issue where these two attributes are constantly at odds. You often cannot have high security if you want maximum efficiency, and you cannot have high efficiency if you prioritize maximum security. This is why banking systems are often criticized for being slow—it is a direct result of their security protocols.
