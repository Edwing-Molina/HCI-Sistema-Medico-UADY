# Usability Principles 

Nielsen's heuristics are the foundation upon which thousands of applications and websites have been built using these usability principles, but are they still relevant today?

I believe they remain relevant but must adapt to the new context of AI and emerging technologies. Many concepts are still respected, such as "Match between system and the real world"; numerous real-world concepts are still used in software products today, like the shopping cart concept on websites like Amazon or Mercado Libre. In AI, this is applied through natural language to align with users' mental models, such as in "Chat to Explore," where the AI responds in familiar terms.

In current AI tools, they often fail in "visibility of system status" (due to opaque generation processes), but patterns like confirmations for AI errors utilize heuristics such as error prevention and user control.

#### Some examples of how these heuristics are used today are:
Productivity Tools
- Google Docs: Collaborative documents show who is editing what (visibility of system status). Shortcuts for experts, yet simple menus for beginners (flexibility).

- Error messages like "no internet connection, retry" in plain language (error recovery).

Social Media
- WhatsApp or Instagram: "Typing..." or "Seen" notifications (visibility). Universal emojis and stickers that everyone understands (standards).

In conclusion, many heuristics are key to the usability of modern software, making it vital to consider them during development. We often focus solely on functionality and meeting deadlines, but we must also know how to integrate usability into our software so that our apps or websites can be used by more people.
