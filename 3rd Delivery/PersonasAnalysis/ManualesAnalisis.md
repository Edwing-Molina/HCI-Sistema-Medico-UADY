# Analisis Manuales

Here is the translation of the usability report into English, using standard UX terminology:

## Comprehensive Usability Analysis: Medical System

This report evaluates the interaction of a user group primarily belonging to the manual, and maintenance sectors. This demographic segment is characterized by variable digital literacy (ranging from independent to highly assisted) and presents very specific patterns of interaction with technology.

### 1. Quantitative Evaluation: System Usability Scale (SUS)

The group's average score provides a clear overview of the system's viability. On a scale from 0 to 100, where 68 is the minimum acceptable industry standard:

* **Scores obtained:** 100.0, 97.5, 72.5, and 40.0.
* **Group Average:** 77.5 / 100 (Usability level: Good)

**Metric Interpretation:**
The 77.5 average indicates that the application's base architecture works. However, the huge standard deviation between the highest scores (100) and the lowest (40) highlights a critical digital divide. The system is extremely easy for those who have mastered their mobile devices, but it becomes hostile and unacceptable for users who rely on family members' help for everyday technological tasks.

### 2. Interaction Patterns (Successes and Frictions)

By observing how users navigated the requested tasks, clear trends were identified:

**What works excellently (Successes)**

* **Institutional Trust:** Despite technical barriers, all evaluated users perceived the Microsoft/Office 365 login gateway as highly professional, official, and secure.
* **Linear Flow (Progressive Disclosure):** The step-by-step appointment scheduling process (selecting a date to reveal available doctors) is intuitive. No user had trouble interacting with digital calendars or dropdown menus once inside the form.
* **System Comprehension:** Business rules are understood naturally. If the calendar prevents selecting a past month, or if the screen goes blank for a few seconds (network latency), users assume this is normal behavior rather than an error.

**Bottlenecks (Critical Frictions)**

* **Login (Mechanical Barrier):** Entering emails and complex passwords using a mobile virtual keyboard is the biggest pain point. It generated multiple typos, task restarts, and, in cases of lower digital literacy, a complete block requiring moderator intervention. Surveys confirmed this step causes "frustration" and confusion.
* **Exploratory Navigation (Cognitive Barrier):** The highest risk of abandonment occurs after scheduling the appointment. Users successfully finish the process on the main screen (thanks to the prominent button), but get completely lost when trying to find their scheduled appointment details in other tabs. Navigation abandonment rates of 50% to 80% were recorded at this specific stage.

### 3. Demographic Context and Accessibility

Initial surveys yielded data that explains user behavior during the test:

* **High Motivation to Use:** All evaluated users confirmed they would use the application frequently. The main reason is dissatisfaction with the current system, citing severe issues like "long waiting lines" or "they don't answer the phone." The need for the tool outweighs the fear of using it.
* **Visual Restrictions:** 75% of the sample reported using glasses, having difficulty seeing their phone screen, or having had to increase their device's font size.
* **Interface Preferences:** The majority agreed they prefer applications with "few, large, easy-to-find buttons" and larger-than-normal text.
